# Using the assistant

[Documentation index](../README.md). Current reference; dated repair evidence lives in [History](history.md).

## Telegram and the dashboard

**No Telegram?** Telegram is optional. Install the dashboard as an app and turn on its notifications: cards, reminders, results and the weekly recap arrive as phone notifications instead.
- **Android (Chrome):** open the dashboard, tap ⋮ → **Add to Home screen** (or **Install app**), open The Machine from your home screen, open **Settings** (the gear at the top) → **General** and tap the bell.
- **iPhone/iPad (iOS 16.4 or newer, Safari):** open the dashboard, tap **Share** → **Add to Home Screen**, open The Machine from your Home Screen (notifications only work there, not in a Safari tab), open **Settings** (the gear) → **General** and tap the bell.
- Turn Telegram off or back on any time: `assistant telegram off`, `assistant telegram on <your Telegram user ID>`. `--remove-bot` with off also deletes the bot and its token from OpenClaw. Without Telegram, **Change** is typed on the dashboard card.

### Calendar

The **Calendar** tab shows the next seven days: meetings from your connected calendars (with their prep card when there is one), cards due or coming back that day, handoff check-ins and promises with a date (the same promise once; a plan made together reads "With …", and one about a meeting that day with the same person sits on that meeting's row). An event on someone else's calendar shared with you is marked with their name and muted; one on both calendars shows once, as yours. All-day and multi-day events show as All day with their dates. Overdue cards and promises come first. A row opens its card's chat, the Promises list, or the meeting in its own calendar. Looking changes nothing. On a computer, **Week** shows the same days as columns.

### Work hours

One setting for when The Machine works with you: on the dashboard under **Settings** (the gear at the top) → **Work hours** (phone-friendly), with `assistant hours`, or as `work_hours` in `config.json`.
- **Work days** (default Monday–Friday): work cards (from `work:` sources) wait for a work day, and snoozed work and returning email threads come back on one. Personal cards keep their timing.
- **Messages from / until** (default 08:00–23:00): nothing is sent outside these hours, to Telegram or the phone; it waits until they start. Reviews keep running in the background. A range like 20:00–04:00 wraps past midnight.
- **Snoozes land at** (default 09:00): where Tomorrow, 3 days and 1 week land, and when a snoozed email thread comes back.
- **Morning digest** (`digest_at`, default 08:30) and **time zone** (`timezone`; the installer takes this computer's, and the dashboard offers the device's when it differs).

Saved from the dashboard, it takes effect at once and is written to `config.json` (the old file stays as `config.json.bak`).

**Telegram** uses OpenClaw's existing bot and message route, never a second poller.
- The outbox records intent before sending. A send with an unknown outcome is held as `uncertain` and reconciled against the chat; it is never resent blindly. After 30 minutes with no sign of it, the card says so and you may tap Send again yourself (`SEND_RETRY_AFTER`). A refused send says "Last attempt failed" with the reason and can be retried at once.
- Only a draft's own attempts lock it. When a card reopens with a new draft after an earlier reply went out, you can edit and send the new one, and Send sends exactly the edited text. While another draft of the card is going out (at most 90 seconds), an edit waits.
- Cards and reminders go out only within your [work hours](../README.md#work-hours) (by default 08:00–23:00). Non-urgent work cards wait for your next work day (by default Monday after a weekend). Several cards may go out together; only the first buzzes.
- Buttons: **Do it**, **Change**, **Snooze** (2 hours, tomorrow, 3 days, 1 week, pick a date), **Not needed** / Dismiss (with a reason; the next review keeps a reason that holds beyond the card, such as "I do those myself", as a fact, so it stops suggesting that), **Mark done**, **Undo** (five minutes). They edit the existing card instead of sending new messages; a finished Do it sends one short Ready notice. Each row holds up to three buttons that fit a phone's width, in order, so a Send shares its row with Mark done (`controls()` in `assistant.py` and `packRows` in the plugin follow the same rule).
- **Change** opens Telegram's native reply box. Only a reply to that exact prompt, within ten minutes, counts as instructions. If the native reply box is unavailable, reply to the confirmed plain prompt. You do not need a slash command or dashboard visit; the legacy slash command remains compatibility-only.
- A Change that asks for the card itself back later ("remind me about this next Tuesday") snoozes it, the same as **Snooze**: it leaves your list, says "Snoozed until Tue 27 Oct, 09:00" and comes back then. A date without a time lands at your morning. No separate reminder is made for it; a reminder about something else, or a recurring one, still is.
- The weekly recap (`weekly_recap`, off by default) arrives once per week, Fridays from 16:00 local time. It leads with what needs you: promises made to you that are late (who, what, since when) and your own with a date in the next two weeks, each linked to its card when there is one. Then the week ahead, Saturday to Friday (your meetings with other people that aren't routine, or have a card on them; from a shared calendar only those you're invited to, and a meeting on two calendars once, plus cards and promised dates due; a promise to you that is due today is still on time and isn't listed), what changed (the share of cards worth it, the writing rules, voices and filters adjusted, and how many drafts went out as written since), new people by name, and two short lists with one-tap buttons in Telegram: busy automated sources without results, never a group chat you write in or one with only a few people (**Filter** adds an `exclude` rule for that chat, app, channel or sender, **Undo** takes it out again, **Keep** stops asking) and cards snoozed more than once (**Drop**, **Do it now**, **Keep**). Nothing changes until you tap, a tap only changes your own filters or cards and never sends anything, and the recap message updates to show what was done. Behind the scenes (reviews, backlog, token cost) is one line at the end. Without Telegram the recap arrives as a phone notification, without buttons.

**The dashboard** shows the same cards grouped as Needs your decision, Ready for you, Preparing and Coming back later, plus the open [promises](../README.md#promises), with Work/Personal filters, deadlines, sources and their health, review runs, feedback and learned facts. A card leads with one short status line under its title (where it stands and what's left for you, such as "Needs you: approve the reply", "Ready for you to check and submit" or "Looks handled: …"), shows a few lines of its text with the rest behind **Show more** and **Read result**, and links another open card it names by its title. When that line is a question for you, or a run stopped to ask you something (not a sign-in), **Answer** leads, also on a card a teammate handed back: it opens a box under the question, and your answer goes back like a **Change** note so the work carries on (on Telegram, Answer asks for a reply). A Change or answer after a handoff came back makes the card yours again. A run that stops for your input names it as short, plain questions, with quick picks for a clear choice that fill the answer box; small calls with an obvious default it makes itself. After a Try again that came back asking the same, Try again is no longer offered. It shows its main action and two more; the rest, such as **Hand off** to a teammate in a Fleet, sit under **More**, and **Not needed** is one compact choice. When reviews are off, the dashboard says so, with the way to turn them on (and, when cards are already there, that they came from the one-time first look). Any signal, on a card or in the Events and Graph tabs, opens in a built-in reader: the full email with its images and attachments (fetched read-only), or the chat, Slack or calendar text, so you don't need the original app; the cards on screen read their email ahead, so it opens at once (the service keeps the last 40 emails it read for 10 minutes, in memory). A Preparing card that is on the browser shows that tab live (see [Sources](sources.md)). At inbox zero you can poke the sleeping robot; poke it a few times and it offers a 20-second mini game, Catch the Zzz. It can be installed as a phone app and sends a push notification for each card, with or without Telegram. When the Machine has a browser view, a quiet **Browser** pill beside Settings opens it: on a computer in the main area next to the sidebar (with **Open in new tab ↗**, and the session keeps running while you look at something else; `#browser` opens it too), on a phone in a new tab. **Settings** (the gear at the top right) has tabs, a pill row like Fleet's: on a Machine linked to an account at app.themachine.computer (hosted, or self-hosted and paired with the Network) **Account** comes first: your plan and its status, the next invoice (excl. VAT), the server's size and region, the Google and Microsoft accounts connected there and your Fleets, with buttons that open that page of the account in a new tab (on a hosted Machine already signed in, through a one-time link that works once, for a minute, in that browser only; Stripe's billing portal opens at Stripe) and, on a hosted Machine, **Sign out** (it signs you out of the account too); **General** has this device's light mode and phone notifications and a link to your Fleet rules when you are in a Fleet; then **Work hours** ([work hours](../README.md#work-hours)), **Writing** (email signatures and **How you write**: a few lines per channel that drafts follow, read from your own messages and kept up to date by your notes and edits; edit or remove any), **Skills** and **Model**. A tab with nothing to show is left out, and the last one you opened is kept. Links open one: `#settings`, `#settings/skills` (`account`, `general`, `hours`, `writing`, `model`; `#settings=skills` works too). Connections stay under Connections. Graph and Connections have sub-tabs like Fleet: Graph has People, Projects, Organisations and Who's who (roles to confirm, counted on the Graph tab too); Connections has your sources by kind (Mail and calendar, Chat, Other sources with Android), **+ Add**, saved Sign-ins and Reviews, and a sub-tab with a source that needs you shows how many and opens first. Links open a tab or sub-tab: `#events`, `#graph`, `#graph=roles` (`people`, `projects`, `orgs`), `#connections`, `#connections=chat` (`mail`, `other`, `add`, `signins`, `reviews`; the old `phone` opens Other sources); the older `#people` and `#health` still work. It is served on `127.0.0.1:<port>` and reached through Tailscale Serve or Caddy; see [Security model](../README.md#security-model).

**Adding a source from the dashboard:** Connections, **+ Add** lists every source this Machine can add and how many of each are connected. Picking one runs the same steps as `assistant connect <source>`, inside the service: its questions show there one at a time (a key in a password field, which only goes to the step that asked for it), then a test read, and the service restarts to start reading it. Google from there uses gog's read-only sign-in in two steps (open the address it shows, then paste the address Google sends you to). Outlook signs in with a device code. Steps that need a terminal (WhatsApp's QR code) say which command to run. On a hosted Machine, Google and Microsoft link to your account page, which signs them in, and the dashboard never shows terminal commands. One source at a time; **Stop** ends it.

**Reconnecting and disconnecting:** a connection whose sign-in expired or was revoked shows **Reconnect** on its card. It runs the same steps for that account, keeping its address, calendar and access: for a Google account gog already holds it asks Google for exactly the scopes that sign-in has now (checked with gog's dry run), never more or less, and when those can't be matched it says which command to run instead. The terminal command stays under **Or use a terminal**. Each card's **Details** also has **Reconnect** and **Disconnect**. Disconnect asks first, takes the account out of `config.json` (the old file stays as `config.json.bak`) and restarts the service; what it already brought in stays, and so do sign-ins and keys, which other accounts may share. On a hosted Machine, Google and Microsoft accounts reconnect and disconnect on your account page.


<a id="card-chat"></a>**Card chat.** Tap a card's title (on a phone; on a computer a click on it renames the card), its quiet **Chat ›** link (a dot marks a card that already has a conversation), its **Asked** note or **See in chat ›** after a Change, and that card opens as a conversation (`#chat=<id>`). A **Change** typed on the card stays on the card; the chat never opens by itself. The header holds back (to the card), the title and a ⋯ menu with the card's other actions and your task list (beside the chat on a wider screen). The thread runs strictly by time in a calm column of about 720px: the Machine's and a coding agent's replies are plain text, your messages are soft pills on the right, and times show on hover. The one open result (a draft waiting on you, an outcome summary or the card's question) is the only raised card; a draft that went out turns quiet with a muted "Sent". It starts with what the card is about: who wrote it, how, and its first lines, with **Read the thread**; for a support conversation, what the other side asked, then a teammate's internal note. Next comes the card itself as the Machine's first message: what is going on and, when it waits on a call, the question in one line ("Your call: …"), with the card's choices under it while it is open. Then each run as one quiet "Checked 5 things" line you can open, followed by what it said and the draft it left, inline with **Show what changed** against the draft before; work handed to a coding agent shows your messages to it ("To Codex") and each answer, its files as chips. What it did (or is still doing) is part of the card from then on: chat replies, later runs and the review build on it, so the next step never repeats finished work. Your own taps are small muted lines, verb first ("Chose Do it", "Snoozed it 5 times", "Sent to Sam"), lined up with the right edge of your messages; a tap you took back within a minute is left out. Replies are redacted like Technical details and leave out the agent's bookkeeping (a learned skill shows only as "Noted for next time", saved files as chips and "Codex sent nothing" as a quiet note, left out when something went out after). A reply that left a page open in the Machine's browser offers **View in Machine browser**. A file a reply points to (a draft, sources) is a chip that opens it, never a bare title. A draft that can't be sent from here (no send path came with it) is the raised result itself, named for what it is ("Internal answer for Andrea · Intercom note"), showing only the draft with its sources behind **Open**, with **Copy draft** first and one line saying why; the card shows the same. Only the draft that went out says "Sent"; one cancelled before it says "Not sent". A send names the person and the app ("Sent to Andrea on Slack"), never a channel or ID. A small muted icon marks where a message came from (email, chat, calendar, note), on the card's source line too. At the bottom are a status line with its one way back ("Snoozed until Fri 09:00 · Unsnooze", "Done · Reopen", "Not needed · Reopen") and a one-line message box that grows as you type. There are no canned suggestions. The box stays above the phone's keyboard, and a refresh while you type keeps your text, caret and focus. A refresh changes only what changed: you stay at the end if you were within a line of it, else where you were reading, and anything you opened stays open. Long titles fit on one line (two on a phone) and fade out, with the full title on hover. A snooze that has ended is back in Ready at once, also before the next refresh. When the Machine can't be reached, the top shows "Offline · retrying" (or "Restarting · retrying" during an update) and it tries again quietly, as soon as the service says; an open page reloads itself after an update. On a wider screen the list and the chat scroll on their own and the page itself stays put; on a phone the view respects the notch and home bar. You can ask about the thread, the people or the context as well as change the draft: a question gets an answer and leaves the draft as it is. A draft written or changed in a chat reply is shown first, never sent in that same reply, also when your message says what to do ("email Sam now"): you read it and tap Send, or say "send it" in a later message. The service checks this itself: the Machine sends for you only a draft that was on the card, unchanged, before the message that approves it. A message is a **Change** on that card: the same path, so the card's status, Telegram, outcomes and learning stay in step (on a snoozed card it brings it back; on a done card it reopens it first), and it continues the card's session with a short follow-up prompt instead of the full card context. While the run goes, the view checks about once a second and shows the reply as it grows. Chat opens only from a card, on a tap (not on the tap that stops a scroll); approvals stay on the card's buttons and you can't add files. The service reads the session through the OpenClaw Gateway's `chat.history` with the pinned official client (`@openclaw/gateway-client`, installed by `npm ci` from `package-lock.json`), as its own operator device with read access only, and takes the source messages, your notes and taps, what went out and each run's saved result from its own database; the browser never talks to the Gateway or holds its token. A message you send from the chat shows on its draft: **Sending…** with a bar, then who it went to, how and when ("Sent to Sam by email · 10:21", and then **Mark done** is the next step), or why it didn't go out with **Try again**.

<a id="new-card"></a>**New card.** Add a card yourself: **New card** under Find a card on a computer (or press **C**, or Ctrl/Cmd+K and type "new"), **+** in the top bar or **New card** in the task list on a phone. Type what needs doing and press Enter; nothing else is needed. Optional chips: Work or Personal, a due date ("Friday", "in 2 weeks", "24 Oct"), a person or organisation from the Graph, and an email to attach. It is a normal card made by the same path as the review's (`card_new`), with the same buttons, chat, snooze, Done, projects and handoffs, and "Added by you" in quiet text. Creating it sends nothing. The next review fills it in (a cleaner title, people and threads, the next step, a draft prepared ahead), but never dismisses it or folds it into another card. Its title and body keep everything you said, and your words open its chat. If the review thinks it is the same as another card, the card asks "Looks like the same as … Merge?" and only your tap merges.

<a id="rename"></a>**Rename.** Give any card your own title: Rename in a card's More menu or the chat's ⋯ menu, a click on the chat's title, a click on a card's title on a computer, or in the sidebar the ⋯ on hover, a double click, a right click or a long press on a phone. Enter saves, Esc cancels, an empty title keeps the old one, up to 200 characters. Your title shows everywhere the card does (the dashboard, Telegram, which edits its message, notifications and a Fleet handoff), and reviews and preparations keep updating the summary and next step but never the title. **Reset to suggested title** goes back to the latest generated one. The chat gets a quiet "Renamed" line.

## Do it

**Do it** queues a preparation: an isolated turn `agent:main:assistant-review-preparation-<job>` of your main agent with `USER.md`, `PREPARATION.md`, the card, your latest note and the full evidence. Up to `preparation_concurrency` run at once and share the OpenClaw browser through a lease. The agent does the work and saves a short result with `prepare` (longer output goes in `memory/assistant/` in the workspace), or records a plain-language blocker. A task it finds already done or no longer needed comes back as "Looks done already" with **Mark done** first. When it only answered your question, explained or made a plan, the card says "Answered. Nothing done yet." and **Do it** leads again. Emails come back as a draft with a **Send** button. In Telegram the card shows the draft itself (to, cc, subject, text), so **Send** there sends exactly that revision and **Edit** opens it in the dashboard; a draft too long for the card, or one where you still pick the recipient, first shows a full preview and sends on **Send now**. Read result on the dashboard shows the draft too. Sends go through the account's Gmail, and the card then says who it went to and when and moves to done (Undo or Reopen bring it back); a handoff instead waits for the reply, and a card with another send still waiting stays in Ready for you. A run interrupted by a crash or outage resumes with its history instead of redoing finished steps.

<a id="prepared-ahead"></a>**Prepared ahead.** When the review is confident you'd say yes, the work starts before you tap anything, and the card arrives done up to the one step that commits something. A reply arrives as a draft with **Send**; a form arrives filled in with **Submit…**, an invoice set up with **Pay…**, a booking picked with **Book…**. That button shows what goes out (amounts, dates, names, text), and **Confirm** runs only that last step, checks that it went through and reports back. When the step posts a message in a web app (a reply in a support inbox, a chat or DM), the card shows its exact text like an email draft, and **Edit** in the Submit dialog (or Telegram) changes it: Confirm then types exactly the edited text, replacing the earlier draft on the page, and checks that it went out. A file it uploads (a receipt with a reply) is listed in that dialog and opens from there; Confirm uploads exactly those files, and when none are listed it says it attaches no file. Once it went through, the card is done. If the page shows something else by then, it stops and tells you instead. Nothing is sent, submitted, paid or changed before your confirm.


## The Machine fixes itself

The service keeps track of its own bugs and can fix them. Small fixes that prove themselves merge on their own and stay undoable for a day; everything else waits for your Merge.

- **What it records.** Every error raised in The Machine's own code (logged by a worker, or a thread that dies) is kept per code location and day: the exception type, its message with quoted text, email addresses, links, long numbers and anything token-like taken out, the file, line and function, how often, and the code version. Never arguments, values or message content. Trouble outside the code (a source or the network down, a full disk, a busy database) isn't recorded as a bug.
- **How it reaches you.** The first `gateway` source turns them into `machine.fault` signals: the first occurrence of a day, then again at 3, 10, 30, 100… Each starts with a plain line: what failed, what you may notice, and whether The Machine can fix it itself; the code locations sit under Details. When an outside service failed (a 5xx, a timeout or a dropped connection, read from where the error came from), it says so instead, that The Machine retries, and that nothing is needed from you; no fix is offered or merged for it. A review suggests a fix when an error recurs or breaks something you rely on. A card about an error nobody acted on closes by itself once newer code has gone a day without it.
- **Do it** never touches the running code (the plugin blocks that). The agent clones it into `fix_dir/<job id>`, finds the cause, fixes it, adds a test, runs the whole suite and commits. The card then has a **Merge fix** button with the summary, the files and the full diff.
- **Merge now** runs the tests again in that clone (`self_test`, by default `scripts/test.py`), checks that neither the running code nor the fix changed since you reviewed it and that the running code has no local changes, then fast-forwards the running checkout to exactly that commit, pushes it when the branch has an upstream (`self_fix_push`) and restarts the service. Anything off means nothing is merged, with the reason on the card. `send_now` can't approve a merge; only the small proven-fix exception below is automatic. A fix whose base moved has to be redone. A change to the OpenClaw plugin takes effect after `openclaw plugins reload personal-assistant-bridge`.
- **Merging by itself:** a fix to one of its own recorded errors merges without a tap when it is small and proven: at most 3 Python files of the service and 50 lines besides the tests, a new test that fails without the fix (checked on a copy of the old code), and no changes to the plugin, AGENTS.md/PREPARATION.md, the database layout or the config. One a day at most. The card says it merged and has **Undo merge** for 24 hours, which reverts the commits, pushes and restarts. It undoes itself when the merged version records the same error again, or any new error within the first hour. `self_fix_auto: false` turns it off; anything that doesn't qualify keeps the Merge fix button. A fix that merged by itself stays on this machine: it isn't pushed upstream (often the public project) unless you set `self_fix_auto_push: true`, and the card suggests pushing it or opening a pull request. A later push never carries along commits that aren't upstream yet, and an undo is pushed only when its fix was.


## Command line

`assistant` (linked by the installer; code in `cli.py`) talks to the running service with the local API token; `connect` and `doctor` work on this machine's files and also run while the service is down. Every command takes `--json`.

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `assistant tasks` | Outstanding tasks; `--later`, `--done` or `--all` for the other tabs |
| `assistant show <id>` | One task (a unique ID prefix is enough) with decisions, feedback and full evidence |
| `assistant promises` | Open promises, yours and those made to you; `--all` adds the ones closed in the last two weeks |
| `assistant search "<text>"` | Full-text search over all sources; `--account <id>` narrows it |
| `assistant events --account <id> --ids 12,13` | Specific signals, or `--query` within explicit accounts |
| `assistant sources` | Connection health per source |
| `assistant reviews` | Review backlog and recent runs |
| `assistant feedback` | Recent decisions and written feedback |
| `assistant prepare <id> --file brief.md` | Save a preparation on an accepted task |
| `assistant preview` | Before activation: one review of the last 7 days; prints the cards, sends nothing |
| `assistant roles` | Your agent reads the first and latest messages with your closest people and notes what each is to you in the graph (also the last step of `assistant profile`; `--again` to run it again right away) |
| `assistant profile` | Your agent drafts how you write and what matters to you from your own messages, and notes what your closest people are to you in the graph; the draft is saved to `USER.md` only after your yes, within the 4,000 characters OpenClaw loads (`--again` for a fresh draft, `--all` to keep every line) |
| `assistant connect [<source>]` | Set up or redo a source (sign-in or key, IDs, a test read, first sync); without a source, lists what still needs connecting. `--account <id>` picks or adds one of several |

| `assistant telegram status\|on <id>\|off` | Telegram is optional: turn card delivery there off (cards then reach you through the dashboard app) or back on. `off --remove-bot` also deletes the bot and its token from OpenClaw |
| `assistant doctor` | Checks the service, OpenClaw bridge, Telegram approval, every source, reviews, dashboard and `USER.md`; every problem comes with its fix. Exits 1 when something is broken |
| `assistant enrich [domain]` | Organisation and work contact data from your enrichment service: status, or look up one domain now |

| `assistant raw <action> '<json>'` | Any other non-owner service action |

## Message actions

**Slack messages and hand-offs.** A Do it result can carry a Slack direct message from your own Slack account (the Maton Slack connection, as your user) to one person, named by Slack `user_id` or work email, with their name. `SlackTransport` in `next_actions.py` is separate from the read-only Slack source and can only look the person up (`users.info`, `users.lookupByEmail`), open the DM, post one message and read that DM back. Before sending it checks that the Slack name matches and that it's an active person, not a bot. An unclear outcome stays uncertain and is checked by finding exactly one message from you with that text in the DM; it's never resent. `delegate:true` (on Slack, or an email to one address) hands the task over: after the send the card is snoozed for `wait_days` (default 3; a weekday at 09:00) with a watch on that person. When they reply (in that DM, or in that email thread or to that email), the card closes itself and their reply is reviewed as an answer to the card; without a reply it comes back saying so. Sending needs a Maton Slack connection that is allowed to post as you. For a teammate in your Work Fleet, the result is a Fleet handoff instead (`kind:handoff`, the message written to them): its **Hand off** button opens the [Fleet handoff](fleet.md) form with that message, and nothing goes before you send it there.

**LinkedIn messages** use the same approval path. The `linkedin` action either replies in a conversation from the task's own LinkedIn signals (`conversation_id`), or writes to a profile slug you gave (`profile` plus `to_name`). Preflight resolves the recipient without sending, and a profile whose name doesn't match `to_name` blocks the send. `LinkedInTransport` runs `linkedin_inbox.py send --yes` with the job ID as token; the reader logs its intent before the one write (fsynced, token admission locked) and never reuses a token. An unclear outcome stays uncertain and is checked through `linkedin_inbox.py verify` by exact message identity, not by matching text or time; missing or damaged send evidence stays uncertain and is never resent. A busy browser just waits.

**X replies and DMs** use the same approval path, through the same signed-in x.com session. The `x` action either replies to a post from the task's own `x.mention` signals (`reply_to`, its tweet ID) or answers in a DM conversation from its `x.dm` signals (`conversation_id`), from the account the signal came in on: plain text, no media, at most 280 characters for a reply (links count 23, as on X; 25000 with `long_posts`) and 10000 for a DM. Send shows the account, the post replied to or the conversation, and the text. Preflight resolves the post or conversation without writing. `XTransport` then runs `x_inbox.py send --yes` with the job ID as token. A reply goes through X's own composer (`x.com/intent/post`): the tab first checks the composer holds exactly the approved text for the approved post, then clicks Reply once, and blocks every other post request. A DM goes to the DM inbox API (`dm/new2`) with a request ID derived from the token. The reader logs its intent before the one write (fsynced, in `~/.local/share/x-inbox/sends.jsonl`) and never sends a token that is already in the log; it switches accounts only under its run lock and always switches back. An unclear outcome stays uncertain and is checked through `x_inbox.py verify` (the returned post ID, else that account's reply to the post with the same text; for a DM the request ID), never resent. Encrypted X Chat conversations are refused. By hand, `x_inbox.py send --handle <h> --primary <p> --reply-to <id> --text '…'` only previews; `--check` also fills X's composer without posting; only `--yes` sends.

**WhatsApp messages** use the same approval path, and only from a card: the owner's Send now, never `send_now`. The `whatsapp` action replies in a chat from the task's own `whatsapp.message` signals (`chat_id`, from the account the message came in on): plain text, no media, at most 4000 characters. `WhatsAppTransport` checks read-only first (the linked number still matches `identity_sha256`, the chat is in the store), then makes the one writable wacli call, `wacli send text --to <chat> --message …`, without `--read-only`; every other wacli call stays read-only. With the sync running, wacli hands the send to it. wacli has no send token, so an unclear outcome stays uncertain and is checked by finding exactly one message from you with that text in the chat newer than the persisted pre-send message inventory and dispatch timestamp; it's never resent. A busy store waits.
