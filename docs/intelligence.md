# Context, people, projects and learning

[Documentation index](../README.md). Current reference; dated repair evidence lives in [History](history.md).

## People and projects

A living map of who you deal with and what's going on, across every account. `graph.py` rebuilds it from scratch every ten minutes (and soon after a label or merge) as a pure function of the signals, your card decisions, project labels and merges, so a rule change also applies to the past. It needs no setup and nothing leaves the machine (unless you turn on [organisation data](../README.md#organisation-data-optional-enrichment)); a rebuild over 5,000 signals with probable links and unnamed work takes under half a second.

- **People** come from identifiers only: email addresses (Gmail, Calendar, Intercom, Salesflare contacts), WhatsApp numbers, Slack users, LinkedIn and X conversations, GitHub logins. Your own addresses (configured, plus any address your sent mail comes from), shared role addresses at your domains (info@, support@), your own name on other channels, newsletters (Gmail Promotions/Updates/Social/Forums, no-reply and mailing senders, unless you ever wrote to them), bots and mass mailings (over 20 recipients) are left out.
- **Who is who:** Slack users are looked up once a month (`users.info`, a few per Slack page); their email joins them to their mail. Names are only names (what people or their tools add is dropped: "Kim Lee (Initech)", "Ann Bell | Acme", "Sam at Globex", "via LinkedIn", emoji, a degree; "Doe, Jane" becomes Jane Doe). The same full name on two different channels is one person, unless two people on one channel carry it. The agent's `graph` `merge` or `different` (two person ids) overrides either way.
- **Same person?** Identities join by themselves only on strong evidence: the same full name on two of them in one message or conversation (someone writing from work with their own address in CC), the same LinkedIn profile found for both by a lookup, their WhatsApp number in mail they wrote themselves (not in quoted mail) with a matching name, their Slack profile's address, or the same full name with one person carrying it per channel. A name alone never joins two identities on one channel. Weaker evidence is only a question: the same full name that two people on one channel carry, or a similar name ("Ann" and "Ann Lee", "Alex" and "Alexander Lee", a nameless jane.doe@ and Jane Doe) with something shared besides (the same company domain, people seen with both, a project, the same role). The person page asks it quietly ("Same person as …?") and People lists them under **Same person?**, each with **Merge** and **Not the same**; Not the same is remembered (`graph_merges` `same=0`). A merged person keeps every identity, signal, role, note, project and photo, since all of those hang on the identities and the graph is rebuilt from them. Their page lists why they are one (**Joined from N identities**), each join with **Undo**, which splits them for good; an old link to one of their identities opens the merged person. Someone deleted permanently is never merged or asked about again. The build keeps the questions in `graph_same`.
- **Closeness** adds per day of contact, capped at 1 a day, so a burst of chat messages is one day: you writing 0.6 (split over recipients), them writing to you 0.3 (0.15 by email until you've written to them), a meeting 0.5 (split over attendees), present in a group or CC 0.05–0.15. Card decisions add once per card for the people it was directly about: Do it, Send or Mark done +1, a Change note +0.5, Not relevant or Wrong facts −0.5 ("Already handled" counts neither way). Everything fades by half every 60 days (`strength × ½^(days/60)`); below 0.01 it isn't stored. Labels: close (3+), regular (1+), occasional (0.3+).
- **Rhythm:** a contact day is one with contact between you and them (a message either way, a meeting), counted in your timezone (`timezone` in config); signals they're merely on, like a teammate answering a customer, don't count, and "last contact" follows the same rule ("last seen" for someone never in direct contact). each person's usual gap is the median of their last eight gaps between contact days. Someone you wrote to on at least two days has **gone quiet** when the silence is longer than three times their usual gap and at least two weeks (and under a year). Future meetings show as "next meeting".
- **Projects** are named by the review agent (`finish_analysis` `projects: {"name": [event_ids]}`, `suggest` `project`) and with `graph` `project` + `event_ids`; `begin_analysis` returns the live names to reuse. A label spreads to the rest of its thread (email thread, Slack thread, Intercom conversation, GitHub issue, recurring meeting; chats within two days). Heat is 0.2 per signal plus your card decisions, fading by half every 21 days; the state compares now with two weeks ago (heating up, active, cooling, dormant). `close_project` ends one; naming it again reopens it.
- **Boards:** projects of one kind in stages (partner listings, onboarding, support escalations), shown on Graph › Projects as a board; Board is the default view and List is one click away, remembered on that device. A review moves a project with `finish_analysis` `stages: [{"project", "board", "stage", "reason", "event_id", "next", "org"}]` (`event_id` from that batch, `reason` one short clause, `next` the owner's next step, `org` a domain for the logo). Only the owner makes a board: from scratch (the board's ⋯ menu, New board) or by saving a suggestion. When at least three open projects on no board are clearly of one kind (`begin_analysis` `off_boards`), a review suggests one board for them once with `graph` `suggest_board: {name, stages, about, projects, reason}`, stages that fit that work (hiring: Sourced, Screening, Interviews, Offer, Hired), judged from what the projects are, not the words in their names. Projects shows it as one calm "Start a board for Hiring?" with its stages, Save, Edit and Not now; nothing is a board until saved (`board_suggestions`). The editor sets the name, stages (rename, reorder, add, remove, up to 8), the optional Stalled or declined row, a line on which projects belong (`about`) and the projects on it. A saved board's projects wait as "Sorting from your messages" until a review places them (`boards` `to_sort` in `begin_analysis`), and later projects that fit its `about` join it. A removed stage's projects go to the nearest earlier stage, and the toast says so. Deleting a board keeps its projects. A review judges what a message means, never its wording, and never moves a project only because time passed. Each move keeps its reason and message (`project_moves`); the tile shows "Moved to In review: … · Fri 23 Oct" and opens that message. The owner drags a tile, or picks a stage from its ⋯ menu on a phone, and that move stands until a message newer than it moves the project again; an older message never undoes a newer move. A board can have a "Stalled or declined" row. A tile shows how long the other side hasn't answered since the owner wrote ("No reply in 14 days", from 10 days) and links an open card about it. The board only shows: it never sends anything. Tables: `boards` (with `about`, `side`; boards made before this are saved boards), `board_suggestions`, `project_moves`, and `board_id`, `stage`, `stage_reason`, `stage_event_id`, `stage_by`, `stage_at`, `next_step`, `org` on `projects` (added on start; back up the database before upgrading).
- **Links:** people seen together on a signal (2–8 people, half-life 45 days), a person's part in a project (0.3 per signal, 45 days), organisations by non-freemail domain (named from CRM accounts when known).
- **Where someone works:** their work email domain, or what a source states plainly: their LinkedIn headline (kept on the person as `headline`, shown as is), an email signature, or them saying where they work in their own message. A review or the organisations pass reads it and sets `graph` with `node_id`, `org` (the name as stated, or an `org:…` node_id), `event_id` (their signal) and `quote` (the words that say it, copied from that message or from their headline on it); the service refuses a quote that isn't in either, and the owner's own messages. It is kept in `person_orgs` with where and when it was said (`via`: LinkedIn headline, email, LinkedIn…), and the person page shows "At Acme · from their LinkedIn headline, Oct 15" with the line and a link to the organisation. A stated name joins the organisation with that name or domain (Acme is acme.com), or stands as its own (`org:named:acme`, no domain, never looked up or hidden as warm-up); the owner's own company marks a colleague instead. A work address wins over a review's statement; your own word (Edit or Add on the person page) wins over both, and an empty one clears it. Nothing else is read from it: no seniority, no role. Reviews get up to five people with a headline and no organisation, each once a month; one organisations pass (`profile_runs` kind `orgs`) runs once per install over the people already in the graph.
- **Probably part of a project:** a signal no label reached that reads like a labelled one (local embedding cosine ≥ 0.72) and shares a person with it is shown as probably part of that project, at half weight (heat 0.1, a person's part 0.15). Meaning alone isn't enough, since it groups by genre (every partnership email reads like every other one). Meetings are left out (a bare calendar title reads like every other one with the same people), and so are closed projects. Review batches show the guess as `probably_project`; a review that agrees names it in `finish_analysis` `projects`, which makes it a normal label. The Graph tab marks those signals "probably part of it". It needs the local embeddings (see Related signals).
- **Unnamed work:** threads from the last 45 days with no project that read alike (cosine ≥ 0.75 between thread averages) and share a person form a group when it spans three or more threads, at least two of them not meetings, and you wrote or met in one. Chats are left out: they read alike per person, whatever the topic. A person on more than 150 threads links nothing. Up to two groups go into a review prompt as "Possibly one piece of work nobody named", each once, again only after it has doubled; the review names it with `graph` `project` or skips it.
- **Roles** (what someone is to you): only what you say on the dashboard (Graph tab, a person, Edit) or what a review reads in a message (`graph` with `node_id` and `role`, a few words: "accountant", "CFO at Globex"); your word stays until you change it. Sources don't set one: being in the CRM, paying through Stripe or sharing a surname says too little about what someone is to you. Instead each review gets up to five of the strongest people with no role (closeness 0.5 and up, strongest first, each asked again after 30 days) with those facts as hints, reads their messages and sets a role only when the messages show it; the dashboard marks those "a review read it in your messages". Onboarding does the same up front in its own turn: after the `USER.md` draft, `assistant profile` starts a roles run for the 15 closest people with no role. The service hands it each person's closeness, organisation, projects, hints and their first and latest direct messages with you (long emails keep their start and end, where a signature is; meetings as titles), three people per page, and the turn sets roles with `graph`. `assistant roles` runs only that step, for example after connecting more sources. Stripe events add their customer as a faint contact and name the organisation when the CRM doesn't. Roles show on people in the Graph tab, in preparation context, `graph` results and the recap's gone-quiet line.
- **Roles, kept honest:**
  - **Evidence and Who's who:** a review gives the message it read a role in (`event_id`, one of that person's own signals) and a short `quote` copied from it; the service refuses a quote that isn't in the message. The Graph tab's **Who's who** sub-tab (after People, Projects and Organisations, with the number waiting, also on the Graph tab itself) lists the review roles you haven't confirmed, closest first, with that line and Read: **✓ Right** makes it your word, Edit changes it. **✓ All N are right**, above the list, confirms every one of them at once after one check. The weekly recap counts the ones waiting.
  - **Learning from corrections:** when you replace a review's role, `role_corrections` keeps the old and new one; the roles run and the review prompt get the latest eight as examples of what you mean by a role.
  - **Just in time:** people on open cards with no role are asked first in reviews (even when faint); `suggest` answers with `no_role_yet` for the people on a new card, and a Do it job's context names them, so whoever has the evidence open can set it.
  - **Organisations:** a role per organisation too (`graph` `node_id` `org:…`: "accounting firm", "customer", "supplier", "possible acquirer"), set by the roles run, reviews or you on its page. People there carry it in preparation context ("at Globex (customer)"), and the roles run sees the roles of others at the same organisation. Each review gets up to two organisations with no role, with their people's roles and their projects; one is asked again as soon as one of its people gets a role, or when its last ask didn't show its people's roles yet, not only after 30 days.
  - **Rechecks:** a review's role is offered again (at most three per review) when it was read more than 90 days ago and there was contact since, when new hints showed up since (now billed through Stripe, say), or when it was saved without its line (roles from before this existed). The review sets it again, the same or a new one. Your own roles are never rechecked.
  - **Relations:** `graph` with `node_id`, `related_to` and `relation` ("assistant of", "sister of", "introduced by", read "<node_id> is <relation> <related_to>") links two people (`graph_relations`, a `relation` link in the graph). Both people's pages show it with Remove; your word stays.
  - **Email warm-up traffic:** when a review or roles pass reads that an organisation's mail is only a warm-up tool's templated exchanges, it sets `graph` `warmup` on it (`hidden_domains`). That domain and its people leave the graph (Organisations, Who's who, the people on cards) and aren't looked up by enrichment. The Graph tab lists them under "Hidden as warm-up" with Undo; your undo stays.
  - **Used:** preparations write to each person as you would to who they are; open cards on the dashboard sort by deadline, then by who they're about (closeness, +2 when their organisation pays through Stripe), so a cofounder or a paying customer comes before a stranger; the recap's gone-quiet line falls back to the organisation's role ("at Globex, customer").
- **Names:** Rename on a person's or project's page in the Graph tab, or Edit on an organisation's (owner only). A person's or organisation's name is kept in `graph_names` and wins over the names in their messages; an empty one gives that back. A project is renamed in place (cards keep their project by name and follow); renaming it to another project's name joins the two.
- **Fleet, Family and Friends:** someone in one of your [Fleets](fleet.md#in-your-graph), your Family or your Friends gets a small marker on their Graph row and page ("Has a Machine · Work Fleet (Acme) · Friend"), with **See in Fleet** and, for a Work Fleet, **Ask your Fleet**; the Fleet tab links each member back to them (**In Graph**). A name from the messages that is only part of one ("kim", all lower case, an address) shows the name they share in that Fleet instead, with **Keep** to make it yours. It is matched on their verified addresses in the Network directory and read when the Graph is shown (`graph.fleet_marks`, `graph.with_fleet`), never asked of their Machine; when they leave, the marker and that name go, and a name you kept or gave stays.
- **Open between you:** a person's or project's page (`graph` `node_id`, the Graph tab) lists open promises both ways (Done and Drop on the dashboard) and open cards on their signals (for a project also cards with its name). Preparation context adds one "open:" part per person and project, leaving out the task's own card.
- **Known about them:** a fact is about its subject, never about whoever sent the message it came up in. A fact has the graph nodes it is about (`fact_about`), set by a review with `remember_fact` `about` (node ids) or later with `remember_fact` `id` and `about`; you are never a node, so a fact about you stays in your own memory (Feedback, What I've learned) and shows on nobody's page. A condensed fact keeps who its originals were about. The page lists them apart from what is open, each with **Not about them** (takes only that link away, for good) and **Forget** (deletes the fact everywhere).
- **The page itself:** the dashboard's header and tabs stay, with a breadcrumb back (Graph › People › name). Each page has its own link, `#graph/<node id>`, and the browser's back button works. Its events come by day, coming up first, each with its source, who wrote it, the time and the start of the text.
- **Tables:** `projects`, `event_projects`, `identity_names`, `graph_merges` (with `source`: the owner, or a review), `graph_same` (recomputed), `person_roles` (with `event_id`, `quote`, a `hints` snapshot and `checked_at`), `role_corrections`, `graph_relations`, `graph_names`, `graph_offers` and `graph_role_asks` hold what was said, offered or asked; `graph_nodes`, `graph_links`, `graph_events` (complete retained membership with a `probable` flag; readers display at most 40 recent signals), `graph_unnamed` and `graph_state` are recomputed. `suggestions.project` keeps a card's project.
- **Uses:** the agent's `graph` action (no args: overview; `query`; `node_id` with links, recent signals and spreading activation two hops out; `event_ids`), a "People and projects" block in each Do it job message (who the people on the task are, their rhythm, projects and who they're often with), the dashboard's Graph tab (`/api/graph`, `?q=`, `?node=`), and the weekly recap.


## Related signals (optional, local embeddings)

Off unless you set it up. Full-text search finds the same words; this finds the same *meaning*: a reply worded differently, the same topic in another source, an answer that already came in. Reviews and Do it runs get a `related` action that looks up signals by meaning, only within the accounts the call names (checked like every other read), and a Do it job message ends with a short "Possibly related" list from the task's own accounts when there are close matches. **Nothing leaves your machine:** signal text goes only to an embedding server you run on `127.0.0.1`, never to a model provider.

**Setup.**

1. Run an embedding server on loopback. Any OpenAI-compatible `/v1/embeddings` endpoint on this machine works (one that also lists its model in `/v1/models`); the tested one is llama.cpp's `llama-server --embedding` with an EmbeddingGemma GGUF, for example `embeddinggemma-300M-Q8_0.gguf` from [`ggml-org/embeddinggemma-300M-GGUF`](https://huggingface.co/ggml-org/embeddinggemma-300M-GGUF) (about 330 MB). If OpenClaw's local memory search is on, it already has both: the model file it uses (`memory.search.local.modelPath` in `openclaw.json`, often in `~/.cache/qmd/models/`) and a bundled `llama-server` under `~/.openclaw/tools/llama.cpp/`. Reuse them rather than downloading again.
2. [`deploy/embeddinggemma.service`](../deploy/embeddinggemma.service) is an example systemd user unit: bound to `127.0.0.1:8792`, `Nice=10`, low CPU and IO priority, `MemoryMax=2G`. Set the binary, model path and name in it (or in `~/.config/embeddinggemma.env`), then `systemctl --user enable --now embeddinggemma`. Check it with `curl -s 127.0.0.1:8792/v1/models`.
3. In `config.json`: `"embeddings": {"url": "http://127.0.0.1:8792", "model": "embeddinggemma-300m", "dims": 256}`. `model` must be the name the server reports (llama-server's `--alias`); the service refuses a server that serves another model. Optional `floor` (cosine for signal-to-signal matches, default 0.6) and `query_floor` (for a text query, default 0.4). The installer adds this block with `enabled: false`: set that to `true` (or drop it), then restart the service.
4. Embed what you already have: `python3 assistant.py embed-backfill` (oldest first, with progress; `--batch` sets events per call). It's safe while the service runs; the service itself embeds new and changed signals in the background, newest first.

**How it works.** `event_vectors` holds one normalized float32 vector per event version (Matryoshka-truncated to `dims`), deleted with its event; changing `model` or `dims` re-embeds. The embedded text is the title plus the first 2,000 characters of the body, without quoted earlier messages. `assistant_signals related` takes explicit `account_ids` and `event_ids` and/or a `query`, `limit` up to 20 and optional `exclude_event_ids`, and returns the `events` shape plus a `similarity`; the given events themselves are never returned. With a query, the meaning ranking is fused with full-text ranking. `events` with `semantic:true` and a `query` uses the same fused ranking. `account_ids:["all"]` searches every configured account (for `events` and `related` only; reviews still name their accounts). The dashboard's Events tab searches All accounts by default, by meaning and words together, and labels each result with its account. If the embedding server is down, the service logs `embeddings error=<class>` and retries after five minutes; queries still get keyword matches (`semantic:false`), and nothing else is affected.

**What was learned, per task.** With embeddings on, the facts and draft rules you taught it get a vector each too (`fact_vectors`), and once there are more than a few, a Do it run gets the ones learned on its card plus up to 8 closest in meaning to it (cosine 0.3 or more), not the whole list. Without embeddings it gets the newest that fit.

**Costs.** No GPU needed. On CPU the server embeds a few signals per second; a first backfill of 5,000 signals takes about 30–40 minutes on a 4-core desktop CPU, and each new signal well under a second. The model takes about 0.3–0.5 GB of RAM while the server runs and no CPU when idle. On a small 2-core arm64 server (October 2026) it was slower and bigger: about 130 tokens a second on one core (a few thousand signals take a few hours once), and 370 MB idle growing to 850 MB while embedding with `--no-repack`, or 300 MB more without it (llama.cpp repacks the weights on arm64 for speed). A first `openclaw memory index` (below) took another 940 MB while it ran. Next to OpenClaw and a browser that is tight on 4 GB of RAM; 8 GB is comfortable. Give the unit its own `MemoryMax` and `MemorySwapMax=0`, so when it grows too big the kernel ends it alone and systemd restarts it. Vectors are about 1 KB each at 256 dimensions (5 MB for 5,000 signals). Search is brute-force cosine in plain Python, about 0.1 s over 5,000 vectors.

**OpenClaw's memory search on the same server.** One server can serve both. Point OpenClaw at it as a provider (the model name containing `embeddinggemma` makes OpenClaw add EmbeddingGemma's task prompts), then build its index once at low priority:

```bash
openclaw config set --batch-json '[{"path":"models.providers.machine-embeddings","value":{"api":"openai-completions","baseUrl":"http://127.0.0.1:8792/v1","apiKey":"local","models":[]}},{"path":"memory.search","value":{"enabled":true,"provider":"machine-embeddings","model":"embeddinggemma-300m","sources":["memory","sessions"]}}]'
systemctl --user restart openclaw-gateway
nice -n 19 openclaw memory index --agent main
```

An explicit provider fails closed: while the server is down, `memory_search` answers "unavailable" instead of falling back to keywords, so keep the unit's `Restart=`.

**Off again:** remove `embeddings` (or set `enabled: false`) and restart; the `event_vectors` table can stay or be dropped.


## Organisation data (optional, enrichment)

Off unless you set it up. With a key for an enrichment service, The Machine looks up the organisations in the graph by domain and keeps a small profile of each: industry, size, location, founded, a short description, LinkedIn, website and logo. It describes the company, never what it is to you; that stays a role.

**Setup.** Put the key in a file only you can read (`chmod 600`), then add to `config.json` and restart:

- A preset: `"enrichment": {"provider": "apollo", "key_file": "~/.local/share/personal-assistant/enrichment.key"}`. Presets: `apollo`, `peopledatalabs`, `hunter`.
- Any other service that answers a GET with JSON about a domain: `"enrichment": {"url": "https://api.example.com/companies?domain={domain}", "headers": {"Authorization": "Bearer {key}"}, "key_file": "…"}`. `{key}` can go in the url or the headers; `key_env` reads it from an environment variable instead. The key never goes in the config itself.
- Common response shapes are understood: flat, or wrapped in `organization`, `company`, `data` or `result`, with the usual field names. If yours differs, `fields` adds paths of your own: `{"industry": ["category.sector"], "about": ["profile.text"]}`.

Check it with `assistant enrich globex.com` (looks one up now) and `assistant enrich` (service and counts).

**What is sent.** Only organisation domains from the graph, never free-mail domains, your own domains, names, addresses or messages. Only organisations whose people you deal with: first those you write with both ways or meet, then those you wrote to; never one where people only wrote in (cold outreach) or were only copied, newsletter and no-reply senders, or campaign sender domains (news., email., e. and the like); a paying customer always. Five per pass every ten minutes and at most 50 a day (`per_day`), each looked up again after 90 days. After an error (rejected key, rate limit, unreachable) it waits an hour, and that domain a day; errors never contain the url or the key. Lookups count against your plan with that service.

**Where it shows.** The Graph tab (logo and industry in the organisation list; a summary, the description and links on its page), `graph` results (`about`, `profile`), preparation context ("at Globex (customer; Manufacturing, 51-200 people, Springfield, founded 1989)") and the roles run. Profiles are kept in `org_profiles`. The logo is kept only as a plain `https` address and loads straight from the service's image host, with no referrer, lazily and at a fixed size; the initial shows until it loads and whenever it can't.

**When it's wrong.** Edit on an organisation's page changes its name, website, LinkedIn, industry, size, location, founded and description. What you write is kept in `org_edits`, wins over every later look-up, and an emptied field gives the looked-up one back. While you edit, Cancel and Save sit where Edit was. Only the fields you changed are sent and checked, so a looked-up value longer than a field allows never blocks Save; a field the server refuses shows why under it. An answer about another company counts as not found: when its website isn't the domain's home page (a blog post, a form, a page on a hosting subdomain) and its name shares nothing with the domain. Services match by the website a company page lists, so a one-person page that links to an article on your partner's site would otherwise get your partner's domain. **Wrong company, clear details** drops a bad match and stops looking that domain up; **Look it up again** (or `assistant enrich <domain>`) undoes it.

**Off again:** remove `enrichment` (or set `"enabled": false`) and restart.

## People data

People with a company address get a job title, photo, company, LinkedIn and city and country, from the same service on its own budget. On a hosted Machine it is on, through the control plane like organisations. Self-hosted, it needs `people_url` in `enrichment`, with `{email}`, `{name}`, `{domain}`, `{first_name}`, `{last_name}` and `{key}`. The `peopledatalabs` and `hunter` presets have one (they look people up by address), and Salesflare's lead API gets `…/enrich/people?name={name}&domain={domain}` by itself. `people_fields` adds paths like `fields`. Turn it off in **Settings → Graph → Look up work contacts**.

**Who is looked up.** Everyone in your graph with a full name and a company address, your own colleagues too (their company is yours). Only the technical skips, where a look-up can't work or would be wrong: no full name, only free mail (no company to look in), a shared inbox like info@ or a no-reply sender. A meeting or mail both ways goes first. Hosted, and self-hosted when paired with the Network: up to 3,000 a day (up to 200 per pass on a paired self-hosted Machine); other self-hosted Machines: five per pass every ten minutes, at most 50 a day (`people_per_day` sets your own). A budget of its own next to the one for organisations; hosted, with only their name and company domain sent (never their address). Each is looked up again after 90 days. Once, after this version arrives, everyone already looked up is looked up again, closest first, within the same daily limit (a few thousand people take a few hours); `enrich_prefs` marks it done so it never repeats.

**What is sent and kept.** Salesflare's lead API gets their name and company domain (an address at eu.example.com asks for example.com); services that look up by address get their work address. Gravatar gets only a SHA-256 hash of their work address, for a photo. Only what the dashboard shows is kept, in `person_profiles` on this Machine: job title, company, a photo, a LinkedIn profile address and city and country.

**Photos.** A photo is saved once on this Machine (`person_photos`) and the dashboard shows it from there, so it never expires. Right after each lookup: the lookup's own photo link while it still works (not LinkedIn's stock silhouette), otherwise their Gravatar, if they have one. One try per lookup (`person_photo_tries`), so never more photo calls than the day's lookups. Only a JPEG, PNG or WebP image of at most 200 KB, checked by its own bytes, fetched over `https` from a public address, without redirects. With neither, initials show; when the lookup's link had expired, that person is looked up again after 30 days instead of 90. Look up work contacts off stops the Gravatar tries too.

**Photos from your own contacts.** Anyone without a photo, personal contacts too, can get one from what the Machine is already connected to, with the permissions it already has: Google contacts, other contacts and the Workspace directory through a `gog` sign-in (once a day, read only, the Contacts and Directory scopes it was given; hosted Google sign-ins don't include Contacts, so they're never asked), and the picture on someone's Slack profile, from the profile lookup the Machine already does for their name. A photo the person chose themselves (their own Google or Slack profile) comes before one set on a contact, and generic ones (Google's letter, Slack's default) are skipped. Only links for people in your Graph are kept (`contact_photo_links`), each downloaded once into `person_photos` with the same checks; a link that didn't work is tried again after 30 days at most. These photos stay on this Machine and are never sent anywhere. Not used: Outlook contacts (the Microsoft sign-in has no Contacts permission), WhatsApp pictures (`wacli` keeps none locally; fetching them would be a live WhatsApp request), Intercom avatars (the connector reads conversations only) and Telegram (the owner's own bot, no contacts).

**Misses.** An answer about someone else (a name with nothing in common, or a company at another domain; the same name at another top-level domain, like example.nl for example.com, is the same company) counts as not found. Self-hosted, a miss keeps only why in `person_profiles`: `unknown`, `another person`, `another company` or `nothing to show`.

**Where it shows.** People rows (photo, or initials, and job title), their page (title, place, LinkedIn), `graph` results (`profile`) and preparation context ("job title Head of Sales").

## Deleting someone

**Delete permanently…**, in the ⋯ menu on a person's page, removes them and everything The Machine knows about them: their graph entry, links, aliases and addresses, looked-up details and saved photo, relationship scores, the messages they sent or that were only between you and them, and the notes learned about them (also the lines in the agent's memory notes that name them). Cards that involve them stay, as your own work, with their name and addresses taken out. What stays is a keyed hash of their addresses and other identifiers, so they are not added, looked up or learned about again: new messages from them, or only to them, are not kept. Messages with other people that they were copied on stay. On a hosted Machine the control plane keeps nothing about people to delete.
